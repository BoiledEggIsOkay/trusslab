Model

Load cases and combinations

Loads and support settlements belong to a load case. The case or combination chosen above the canvas is the one analysed; a combination adds up factored load cases and is solved as one action state.

Generated: each member weighs W = ρ·A·L·g (density × section area × length × 9.80665 m/s²), and half of it acts downward at each end node. Recalculated whenever the geometry, a section or a material changes; loads cannot be entered here.

Analysis

λ multiplies every action of what is analysed, once: nodal loads and prescribed settlements alike (a combination after its factors γ). The model is linear, so displacements, forces and reactions scale by λ too. λ = 0 means no actions; a mechanism stays unstable at any λ.

A safe member whose governing safety factor is below the target is flagged (!), not failed.

Assumptions of the analysis

2D, linear-elastic, small-displacement analysis of a pin-jointed truss by the Direct Stiffness Method. Members carry axial force only; loads act at nodes. Buckling uses Euler's Pcr = π²EI/L² for an ideal pin-ended member (K = 1) with the weak-axis I of its section. This is an educational analysis, not a code-compliant design.

Three workspaces, one model

        
    
          
  • Learn: lessons, predicting tension and compression, free-body diagrams of a joint, the method of joints, and the Solver Explorer, which walks through the solver's own matrices.
    • 
          
  • Analyze: build the truss, load it, choose the load case or combination, and read the results.
    • 
          
  • Design: materials, sections, strength and buckling checks over the combinations, a deflection criterion, and section sizing.
    • 
        

        
All three work on the same model and the same solver. Guided opens explanations and worked calculations next to the results; Technical keeps them closed and shows more numbers. Neither changes the model or the analysis.

        
On a phone, TrussLab is view-only: you can open models, inspect them and read results and lessons, but editing needs a tablet or a computer.

Units and sign conventions

        
    
          
  • Global axes: x to the right, y upward, angles anticlockwise from +x in degrees. Coordinates in metres.
    • 
          
  • Loads and reactions are positive in +x and +y, so gravity loads are negative Fy.
    • 
          
  • Axial force, stress and strain are positive in tension (+, T) and negative in compression (−, C). Zero-force members are drawn dashed.
    • 
          
  • Inputs: E in GPa, σy in MPa, A in mm², I in mm⁴. Force and displacement units are set under View. All calculations and all exports use SI base units (m, N, Pa).
    • 
        

      

      

        
Supports

        
    
          
  • Pin: ux = uy = 0.
    • 
          
  • Roller (uy = 0) rolls horizontally; roller (ux = 0) rolls vertically.
    • 
          
  • Inclined roller: rolls on a surface at angle α from +x; the displacement normal to the surface is zero and the reaction acts along that normal. It is handled exactly by rotating the nodal frame (K′ = Λ K Λᵀ), not by a penalty spring. Rotated DOFs are labelled x′, y′.
    • 
          
  • Custom: fix x′ and/or y′ of a frame rotated by α. A custom support with neither axis fixed restrains nothing, and TrussLab warns about it.
    • 
          
  • Settlements: any fixed axis of any support can settle, for example δy′ = −10 mm. A settlement is measured along the support's own axes x′, y′, belongs to a load case like the loads, and is scaled by λ. A settlement on an axis that is not fixed has no effect, and TrussLab warns about it.
    • 
        

      

      

        
Method: the Direct Stiffness Method

        
    
          
  1. For each member: L = hypot(Δx, Δy), c = Δx/L, s = Δy/L (no trigonometric round-off). The global-axis element matrix is
            
    [kᵉ] = (EA/L) ×
              
    c²cs−c²−cs
    css²−cs−s²
    −c²−csc²cs
    −cs−s²css²
    2. 
          
  2. The 2n × 2n global [K] is assembled with Neumaier-compensated summation, so it is exactly symmetric.
    3. 
          
  3. DOFs are split into free (f) and restrained (s): Kff uf = λFf − Kfs(λus), where Ff holds the nodal loads at the free DOFs, us the prescribed settlements at the restrained DOFs, and λ is the load factor.
    4. 
          
  4. Stability is verified before solving: zero-stiffness DOFs are detected, Kff is Jacobi-equilibrated to K̃ = D−½KffD−½, and a rank-revealing (pivoted) Cholesky factorisation tests positive definiteness. A rank deficiency means a mechanism: the solve is refused and the mechanism mode shape is shown. The Maxwell count m + r − 2j, the determinant (in log form, so it cannot overflow), and the 1-norm condition number κ₁ are reported.
    5. 
          
  5. Solver: LU decomposition with partial pivoting, followed by iterative refinement whose residuals are computed with error-free transformations (Dot2) at about twice double precision.
    6. 
          
  6. Load cases and λ: every action, nodal force or prescribed settlement, belongs to a load case. The load factor λ multiplies all actions of what is analysed: {F} → λ{F} and {us} → λ{us}. The model is linear, so u(λ) = λ·u(1), and member forces and reactions scale the same way. At λ = 0 there are no actions: a stable structure has no displacements, member forces or reactions, while a mechanism stays unstable at any λ.
    7. 
          
  7. Self-weight (the generated case): each member weighs W = ρ·A·L·g, with ρ its material's density in kg/m³ (never guessed from a preset; a material without one adds no weight, and the case is then marked incomplete wherever it is shown), A its section area, L its length and g = 9.80665 m/s². For a pin-ended member the end reactions of its own uniform weight are W/2 at each end, so W/2 is applied downward at each end node: the truss stays axial-only, and the member's own bending under its weight is not modelled.
    8. 
          
  8. Load combinations: factors γi on load cases, the self-weight case included. The combined actions F = Σ γiFi and us = Σ γius,i are built first and the combination is solved directly as one action state, then λ multiplies it once. Safety factors, utilisations and statuses are never added across load cases: they are not linear in the loads.
    9. 
          
  9. Reactions: {R} = [K]{u} − λ{F}, where {u} holds λ{us} at the restrained DOFs. Member force: N = (EA/L)[(u₂ − u₁)c + (v₂ − v₁)s]. σ = N/A, ε = ΔL/L.
    10. 
          
  10. Checks: SFyield = σy/|σ|. For compression members SFbuckling = Pcr/|N| with Pcr = π²EI/L². The governing SF is the smaller one. Status is yielded or buckled when SF < 1.
    11. 
          
  11. Verification: global equilibrium ΣFx, ΣFy, ΣM, and nodal equilibrium rebuilt from the member forces, are reported in Results and in the solver log.
    12. 
        

      

      

        
Assumptions and limitations

        
    
          
  • Pin-jointed members with axial force only. Loads act at nodes only: self-weight too, as half of each member's weight at each end node. No distributed loads, temperature, or geometric or material nonlinearity.
    • 
          
  • Linear theory: equilibrium is written in the undeformed geometry. Geometrically unstable configurations (for example three collinear nodes loaded sideways) are reported as mechanisms.
    • 
          
  • Euler buckling assumes ideal pin-ended members (K = 1). If σcr = Pcr/A exceeds σy, yielding governs. Inelastic buckling, imperfections and code resistance factors are not applied.
    • 
          
  • The Douglas fir preset uses 49.9 MPa, the clear-wood compressive strength parallel to grain at 12 % moisture content (USDA Wood Handbook, coast Douglas-fir), as its limit stress. Graded lumber design values are much lower, so edit this for your grade and code. Its density, 540 kg/m³, is an approximate representative value at 12 % moisture content: specific gravity 0.48 (USDA Forest Products Laboratory, Wood Handbook, 2021, Table 5–3b) × 1.12 × 1000 kg/m³ = 537.6 kg/m³, rounded. Enter your own value if you know it.
    • 
          
  • TrussLab is an educational analysis tool, not certified structural design software. A code-compliant design also needs the load combinations of a standard, resistance factors, imperfections, connection design and serviceability rules that TrussLab does not implement.
